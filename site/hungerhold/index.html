Every life teaches the next.

A pixel survival game about one family, from a cave to a kingdom, one generation at a time. Free in your browser, pay what you want.

How it plays

Live, learn, hand it down

You wake in a cave, starving, with no memory. Find water. Strike a spark before the wolves come out. Make it through the night.

Then you die, because everyone does. Your child picks up where you left off, with your traits in their blood and your keepsake in their pack. Generation after generation, your family climbs Maslow's hierarchy of needs, from a cave to a kingdom.

Designed in 2014, built in 2026. A life lasts about 15 to 25 minutes, so one fits an evening.

The world breaks

  • Dig under a ledge and it trickles, then drops. The rubble is easy to dig and sometimes hides a rock.
  • Storms bring gales. A weak tree creaks, sways and comes down, and a lean-to in the open can tear away. You get six seconds of warning.
  • Lightning leaves craters. Floods eat riverbanks. Rain turns soaked slopes to mud.
  • Break things back. A stone pick splits boulders into rocks and cracks old ruin walls.
  • Still fair: every one of these warns you first, and the tombstone says how it happened.

A better first minute

  • Every new family wakes to its own opening: a dying ember, a sleeping rabbit, a storm on the way, or a stranger's cold camp outside.
  • The cave has secrets. Marks on the wall, read one at a time. Sit still long enough and something odd happens. (A raccoon may judge you.)
  • Crafting explains itself. The game tells you the first time you can make something, and what's missing for the rest.
  • The rabbit no longer runs backwards. It was rude.

Little things

  • Achievements now pop up, small and quiet, with 30 new ones for everyday things. "Probably fine": eat the red mushroom.
  • Every homeland has its own sky and far hills, changing with the hour and the weather.
  • Calmer tool sounds, so the warnings stand out. Throwing finally makes a noise.

Plays better, fights fairer

  • A 16:9 window, click to play, and a fullscreen button on the title and pause screens.
  • About forty fixes from playtests. Holding a key when you die no longer skips your tombstone.
  • Fairer boars. The warning lasts the whole windup, running clear calls the charge off, and they give up and go home.

The whole game

What's in it

A world that reacts

  • Fire spreads through dry grass, bushes and your own camp. Wind carries sparks. Burnt land grows back richer.
  • Water flows. Scoop it from the pond with a bark bowl, splash out a blaze, or dig a channel and let the pond do it for you.
  • Dig into layered earth and rock. Tunnels can cave in, and you'll hear them creak first.
  • Weather has causes: dry spells, storms, gales, lightning, floods, hail and quakes, each one warned before it hits.
  • Animals flee fire, and wolves back off from a burning brand. Your family fights fires at camp.

A family that remembers

  • Every tombstone says how you died, and whose fault it was ("burned in the grass fire you lit").
  • Choose your heir from your own children, each with inherited traits. Keep a family tree and a chronicle of every life.
  • Find the graves of your ancestors, and the ruins of families that fell.

From a cave to a kingdom

  • Five tiers: Cave → Cabin → Village → City → Kingdom. Each one automates the chores of the last.
  • Your first camp works from day one: stack a woodpile, give your kin jobs, dry meat on a rack, and follow the goal line to what's next.
  • Hire hunters and foragers, pass laws, make inventions, deal with other peoples, and win one of six Legacies.
  • The Pyramid dashboard shows which of your people's needs are met, with advisors who suggest the next fix.

Lives that fit an evening

  • A life lasts about 15 to 25 minutes, with warnings before the end.
  • "A good place to stop" at every dawn, and a card that shows what each life banked.
  • Save and quit anytime. Continue drops you back exactly where you were.

Play your way

  • Keyboard, mouse, gamepad or touch. On phones, tap to walk.
  • Cozy or Standard pace, key and pad rebinding, high contrast, bigger text and reduced motion.
  • Up to five family houses, each founded in its own homeland: forest, marsh, highland, coast or valley.

Play it

Free in your browser

No download, no account. It's pay-what-you-want, so $0 is fine, and anything more keeps the family growing. Your save lives in your browser; use Move family on the title screen to carry it to another device.

A/D or the arrows to walk, W to jump, E to use, Q to eat, F to fight or use the held item, Z to rest, Esc to pause. Or tap and click to walk.

How this game is made

The code and most of the text are written with AI coding assistants and edited by us. The art and sound are made by people: 99% of the art and 100% of the sound and music come from licensed packs by human artists and composers, all credited in the game (About → Made by other creators). No AI-generated art or music is used.

Pixel artists, composers and sound designers: we're commissioning more human-made work, and every contributor is credited in the game. partners@biosicstudios.com
