How it plays

Live, die, hand it down

You wake in a cave, starving, with no memory. Find water. Strike a spark before the wolves come out. Make it through the night. Every new family wakes to its own opening: a dying ember, a sleeping rabbit, a storm on the way, or a stranger's cold camp outside.

Then you die, because everyone does. The valley warns you first, and your stone says how. Your child picks up where you fell, with your traits in their blood and your keepsake in their pack. Generation by generation, the family climbs from a cold cave to a kingdom.

Roguelikes follow a lone rogue. Hungerhold follows a family. We call the form a kinlike.

What's new in v2.2.1

The ground gives way

  • Dig under a ledge and it trickles, then drops. The rubble is easy to dig and sometimes hides a rock.
  • Storms bring gales. A weak tree creaks, sways and comes down, and a lean-to in the open can tear away. You get six seconds of warning.
  • Lightning leaves craters. Floods eat riverbanks. Rain turns soaked slopes to mud.
  • Break things back. A stone pick splits boulders into rocks and cracks old ruin walls.
  • Still fair. Cave-ins creak twice before they fall, open shafts are marked in the dark, and a pit you fall into gets steps. The tombstone says how it happened.

A better first minute

  • Every new family wakes to its own opening: a dying ember, a sleeping rabbit, a storm on the way, or a stranger's cold camp outside.
  • The cave has secrets. Marks on the wall, read one at a time. Sit still long enough and something odd happens. (A raccoon may judge you.)
  • Making things explains itself. The game tells you the first time you can make something, and what's missing for the rest.
  • At a fire, what you hold decides: a twig feeds it, a rock strikes it, fiber and twigs raise a lean-to.
  • The rabbit no longer runs backwards. It was rude.

Little things

  • Achievements now pop up, small and quiet, with 30 new ones for everyday things. "Probably fine": eat the red mushroom.
  • Every homeland has its own sky and far hills, changing with the hour and the weather.
  • Hold a burning twig to see which way the wind blows.
  • Calmer tool sounds, so the warnings stand out. Throwing finally makes a noise.

Plays better, fights fairer

  • A 16:9 window, click to play, and a fullscreen button on the title and pause screens.
  • Fits every screen. The title, heir picker and tombstone fit any window, and phone buttons stay put.
  • About forty fixes from playtests. Holding a key when you die no longer skips your tombstone, and storm nights are dark but not blind.
  • Fairer boars. The warning lasts the whole windup, running clear calls the charge off, and they give up and go home.
The whole game

What's in it

A family that remembers

  • Every stone says how you died and whose fault it was ("burned in the grass fire you lit"), with a line on what to try next time.
  • Choose your heir from your own children, each with inherited traits. Keep a family tree and a chronicle of every life.
  • Find the graves of your ancestors, and the ruins of families that fell before yours.

Lives that fit an evening

  • A life takes somewhere between ten and twenty-five minutes, with warnings before the end.
  • Every dawn gives you a good place to stop, and a card shows what the life banked.
  • Save and quit any time. Continue drops you back exactly where you were.

The valley warns you first

  • Snakes hiss. A boar paws the ground before it charges. Every rockfall creaks twice. Deep water turns your breath red early.
  • Fire spreads through dry grass, bushes and your own camp, and wind carries sparks. Burnt land grows back richer.
  • Water flows. Scoop it with a bark bowl, splash out a blaze, or dig a channel and let the pond do the work.
  • Dig into layered earth and rock. Wide tunnels creak, crack and cave in unless you prop them with timber.
  • Weather has causes: dry spells, storms, gales, lightning, floods, hail and quakes.
  • Build your way out: a firebreak, a dam, a roof over your fire. When it works, the game tells you: "Your firebreak held. The camp is safe."

A home that outlives you

  • Your first camp works from day one: stack a woodpile, give your family jobs, dry meat on a rack, and follow the goal line to what's next.
  • Five tiers, from Cave to Cabin to Village to City to Kingdom. Each one takes over the chores of the last.
  • Hire hunters and foragers, pass laws, make inventions, deal with other peoples, and win one of six Legacies.
  • A needs board shows which of your people's needs are met, with advisors who suggest the next fix.

Share it

  • Share this life: turn any stone into a picture card, square or tall for stories. Only what's on the card leaves your device.
  • Today's valley: the same world for everyone, every day. Compare how you died.
  • Friend links: send a world or a gift. A friend can visit your valley for one life without touching their own family.

Play your way

  • Keyboard, mouse, gamepad or touch. On phones, tap to walk.
  • A gentle Guide points out each new system as it arrives. You can turn it off.
  • Cozy or Standard pace, key and pad rebinding, high contrast, bigger text, reduced motion, and separate music and effects volume.
  • Up to five family houses, each founded in its own homeland: forest, marsh, highland, coast or valley, each under its own sky.
  • Settings → Art → Classic shows the game as it first looked, drawn entirely in code.

In the works

Coming in v3.0

"It's the same game, and now it's ours."

Our next big update makes the handover the best moment in the game. Everything below is in progress and none of it has shipped yet.

  • A valley with a voice. People talk in short bubbles you can read at a glance. The Elder tells you off for eating raw meat, and you miss her when she's gone.
  • Incidents. A raccoon robs the hearth store because you left the pit dark. A goat eats the lean-to you just built. Your grandson comes home with a wolf pup, and one camp dog per family barks at wolves for lives to come.
  • The handover, done properly. Your stone becomes a Hand down card: the cause, your part in it, the lesson that passes on, and the keepsake you pick. The heir wakes by the hearth with a name and a quiet goal: visit your mother's stone.
  • The lesson speaks. When a lesson saves you, the game says so, once: "Orwam's lesson: listen to the roof."
  • Know-how. At a boulder or a lit fire, the family's know-how shows what you can make there, where to find what's missing, and who taught you each row.
  • Two things to do by hand, if you like. Strike the spark yourself, or stare a wolf down with a burning brand. Always skippable.
  • Every heir their own person. Faces, builds and colours that differ, plus quirks that pass down. The swamp will look like a swamp, and so will the snow and the deep.
  • Streaming mode. One setting, off by default: bigger text, warnings kept inside vertical clips, numbered heir cards so chat can vote, and your valley's code on screen so viewers can play the same world.
  • A better first five minutes, so life one teaches the form before the first death.
  • What stays: no shop and no coin (in a life, food is the only currency, and it rots), no telemetry, and no AI-generated art or music.

No date yet. Some of this may change or be cut if it doesn't hold up in playtests; we'll post progress on itch.io as it merges.

Play it

Free in your browser

Play right in your browser on itch.io, or download the zip, unzip it and open index.html: Windows, Mac or Linux, no install, and it works offline. It's pay-what-you-want: $0 is fine, and $5 keeps the family growing.

Your family is saved in the browser you play in; use Move family on the title screen to carry it to another device. Controls: A/D or the arrows to walk, W to jump, E to use, Q to eat, F to fight or use the held item, S to dig, Z to rest, Esc to pause. Or tap and click to walk.

Credits

How this game is made

The code, most of the in-game text and the tools are written with AI coding assistants and edited by us. The art and sound are made by people: 99% of the art and 100% of the sound and music come from 22 licensed packs by 11 human artists and composers, all credited in the game (About → Made by other creators). Most of that art was edited with AI tools to fit one style, and those pieces are credited as "modified". A few things are still drawn in code (AI-written): the beached whale on the coast (the one sprite no pack had), the sea and cliffs behind the coast, the weather and sky effects, and a few hand-placed pixels. Settings → Art → Classic shows the all-code look. No AI-generated art packs or AI music are used.

Art and sound by Anokolisa, AntumDeluge, Chequered Ink, Glionox, LYASeeK, SMStudios, StumpyStrust, alkakrab, carysaurus, rubberduck and sfelly79. Designed in 2014, built in 2026 by Biosic Studios.

Pixel artists, composers and sound designers: we're commissioning more human-made work, and every contributor is credited in the game. partners@biosicstudios.com
